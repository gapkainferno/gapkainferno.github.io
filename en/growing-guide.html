Growing Guide

Choose the species of your pepper listed on your seed packet, or choose the relevant vegetable.
Capsicum Chinense
The hottest group of peppers in the world! Originating from the Amazon and Caribbean. They have a fruity-citrus aroma and extreme capsaicin concentration. Plants are tall and spreading with textured leaves.
Example varieties:
Carolina Reaper, Trinidad Scorpion, Ghost Pepper (Bhut Jolokia), Habanero, Scotch Bonnet, 7 Pot, Chocolate Bhutlah and more
Heat
100k - 2.2M+ SHU
Maturity
90-120 days
Germination temp
26-30C
Height
80-150 cm
Step-by-step guide
1
Seed preparation
Prepare a warm seedling mat or a heated tray to keep the substrate warm.
2
Sowing (depth 0.5-1 cm)
Use a light seed mix. Temperature 26-30C is critical. Cover with plastic or keep the surface consistently moist. Germination: 7-14 days (Chinense is the slowest among species).
3
Seedlings (16+ hours light)
After sprouting, provide maximum light: 16-18h/day with supplemental lighting if needed. Water moderately; overwatering is worse than underwatering. Transplant early; Chinense likes root space.
4
Planting out (after frost, May)
Spacing 50-80 cm (plants get large). Full sun and wind protection. Add compost and calcium.
5
Feeding
First 2 months: nitrogen for vegetative growth. During flowering: increase potassium and phosphorus, reduce nitrogen. Feed regularly and stake plants because they get heavy with fruit.
6
Harvest (Aug-Oct)
Fruit is ready when color changes fully and texture softens. Always use gloves! Reduce watering two weeks before harvest; heat increases by about 20-30%.
Tips for Chinense
Potassium stress: Reduce watering 10-14 days before harvest to stimulate capsaicin production.
Important for Chinense
These are among the hottest peppers, up to 2.2M+ SHU. Always use gloves. Capsaicin oil can form droplets on fruit; do not touch your face.
Capsicum Annuum
The most common pepper species: fast and forgiving, ideal for beginners. Originates from Mexico and Central America. Includes sweet bell peppers and hotter varieties like jalapeno.
Example varieties:
Jalapeno, Serrano, Cayenne, Hungarian Wax, Shishito, Padron, Bell pepper, Poblano/Ancho and more
Heat
0 - 50k SHU
Maturity
60-85 days
Germination temperature
20-28C
Height
40-90 cm
Step-by-step guide
1
Preparation
Annuum germinates quickly; you can start with the paper-towel method.
2
Sowing (depth 0.5 cm)
Use light seed mix. Temperature 22-28C. Cover until germination. Germination: 3-10 days, the fastest species.
3
Seedlings (14+ hours light)
Provide light from a south window or grow lamp. Water when topsoil dries. Transplant at two true leaves if needed.
4
Planting (mid-May)
Spacing 40-50 cm. Tolerant to soil types but prefer sun. Great in pots on balconies. pH: 6.0-7.0.
5
Feeding
Use a balanced fertilizer regularly. During fruiting, add potassium. Regular watering is the key to large fruit!
6
Harvest (July-September)
They ripen quickly. Jalapeno can be picked green. The longer it stays on the plant, the hotter it gets.
Tips for Annuum
Balcony growing: Annuum peppers are ideal for pots. Jalapeno, Serrano and Shishito grow well in 5-10L containers.

Green harvest: Jalapeno, Shishito and Padron can be picked green for milder heat or red for a hotter, sweeter flavor.
Capsicum Baccatum
"Peruvian peppers" are a group with a unique fruity-citrus flavor. Originating from South America (Peru, Bolivia). Flowers often have yellow or green spots inside, a typical trait.
Example varieties:
Aji Melocoton, Aji Amarillo, Aji Lemon Drop, Aji Panca, Aji Crystal, Aji Charapita, Bishop's Crown, Peppadew and more
Heat
500 - 50k SHU
Maturity
75-100 days
Germination temperature
24-28C
Height
80-120 cm
Step-by-step guide
1
Preparation
Can be germinated on a paper towel or sown directly into seed mix.
2
Sowing (depth 0.5-1 cm)
Light soil, 24-28C and high humidity. Germination: 7-14 days. Patience helps: Baccatum does not rush.
3
Seedlings (16 hours of light)
Give them plenty of light. Baccatum stretches in shade and grows quickly after transplanting.
4
Planting out (May)
Spacing 60-70 cm. They love warmth and sun. Add organic matter; Baccatum appreciates fertile soil. pH: 6.0-6.5.
5
Feeding
Moderate feeding. Potassium during fruiting brings out the citrus flavor. Water regularly, but avoid standing water.
6
Harvest (August-September)
Fruit is ready when it reaches its characteristic color: yellow, orange or red.
Tips for Baccatum
Citrus flavor: Baccatum is known for a lemony-fruity aroma. Pick fully ripe fruit for maximum flavor.

Freezing: Aji Amarillo and Lemon Drop freeze beautifully without losing flavor.

Overwintering: In warm regions Baccatum can grow for several years. Bring it indoors for winter to extend fruiting!
Capsicum Pubescens
"Mountain peppers" from the Andes! The only Capsicum with black seeds and pubescent leaves and stems. Cold-hardy, growing naturally at 2000-3000m. Rocoto and Manzano are Latin American legends.
Example varieties:
Rocoto (Peru), Manzano (Mexico), Locoto (Bolivia), Canario (yellow Rocoto)
Heat
30k - 100k SHU
Maturity
120-150 days
Germination temperature
18-24C (cooler!)
Height
100-200 cm
Step-by-step guide
1
Preparation
Pubescens can take a very long time to germinate, so be patient. Seeds have a thick coat and need time to absorb moisture.
2
Sowing (depth 1 cm)
WARNING: Pubescens does not like high temperatures. Keep 18-24C; at 28C+ seeds may fail. Germination: 7-20 days, the longest among Capsicum.
3
Seedlings (14+ hours of light)
Plenty of light and moderate temperatures around 18-22C. It grows slowly; fuzzy leaves are normal.
4
Planting out (May)
Spacing 80-100 cm for large plants. They can handle cooler climates and may survive +5C. They dislike heat above 30C. pH: 6.0-6.5.
5
Feeding
Give plenty of organic matter: compost and humus. Pubescens is perennial in nature, so it loves rich soil. Water regularly.
6
Harvest (September-October)
They ripen slowly, 120-150 days. Fruit is thick-walled, fleshy and juicy, almost apple-like. Red or yellow fruit can overwinter in a cool room.
Tips for Pubescens
Do not overheat: This is the only pepper species that does not like heat. Above 30C, growth suffers. Ideal temperature: 18-25C.

Perennial habit: You can keep it in a large pot and move it to a cool place for winter (+10-15C). It can live for years.

Important for Pubescens
This is the most challenging pepper species to grow. It germinates slowly, ripens slowly and dislikes heat, but the unique flavor and cold tolerance are worth it.
Capsicum Frutescens
Compact, highly productive peppers from Latin America. Plants are small but covered in tiny hot fruits. Fruits often grow vertically, the basis for classic Tabasco sauce.
Example varieties:
Tabasco, Thai Chili (Prik Kee Noo), African Bird's Eye, Malagueta, Piri Piri
Heat
30k - 100k SHU
Maturity
70-95 days
Germination temperature
24-28C
Height
50-100 cm
Step-by-step guide
1
Preparation (March)
Frutescens germinates well without pre-soak; either sow directly or use the wet towel method.
2
Sowing (depth 0.5 cm)
Light soil, 24-28C, covered tray. Germination: 7-14 days. Faster than Chinense, slower than Annuum.
3
Seedlings (14 hours of light)
Compact seedlings that do not stretch much. Give plenty of light. Suitable for windowsill growing.
4
Planting out (May)
Spacing 40-50 cm. Great for 5-10L balcony pots, and they can become impressive plants in open ground. They love sun and warmth. pH: 6.0-6.5.
5
Feeding
Moderate regular feeding. More potassium means more fruit. Water regularly, but do not overwater.
6
Harvest (July-October)
Very productive: one plant can give 100+ small fruits. Fruit detaches easily and ripens from yellow to red. Pick at any stage.
Tips for Frutescens
Container growing: Frutescens are ideal for pots. Thai Chili and Tabasco grow well on a balcony or windowsill.

Perennial habit: Bring the plant indoors for winter and it can live for years, fruiting almost year-round.

Decorative habit: Fruits grow upright and look striking, with red, yellow and green fruit on one plant at the same time.
Solanum Lycopersicum (Tomatoes)
Kings of the summer garden! From sweet cherries to beefsteak varieties. Tomatoes love sun, space and proper watering, a rewarding crop for true home flavor.
Popular types:
Cherry, Brandywine Pink, Mystery (heirloom), Pink Giant, Bear Paw, Mongolian Dwarf, yellow and black exotic varieties.
Growth type
Determinate / Indeterminate
Maturity
85-120 days
Germination temperature
22-26C
Sowing depth
1.0 - 1.5 cm
Step-by-step guide
1
Sowing (March - early April)
Tomatoes dislike surface sowing. Plant 1-1.5 cm deep in nutrient-rich mix. Cover until first true leaves appear. Germination: 4-7 days.
2
Light and temperature
Move to the brightest location after emergence. Tomatoes tend to stretch; aim for 12-14 hours of light. Optimal temps: day 20-22C, night 16-18C.
3
Pricking out and potting on
When two true leaves appear, pot into 0.5L cups. Tomatoes love deep planting; bury to the cotyledons to promote root growth.
4
Planting in soil / greenhouse
In greenhouse: mid-April. Outdoors: after May 15 (frost risk passed). Spacing 40-60 cm depending on variety.
5
Care and pruning side shoots
Indeterminate types need staking and side-shoot removal. Water at the root and avoid wetting foliage.
6
Harvest (July - October)
Harvest fully ripe for best flavor. If frost is forecast, pick mature green fruit; they will ripen indoors.
Tips for Tomatoes
Boric acid: Spraying during flowering helps set fruit in hot weather.

Mulching: Straw or hay conserves moisture and reduces soil-borne pathogens contacting leaves.
Solanum Melongena (Eggplant)
Demanding garden residents that love steady warmth, moisture and rich soil. From classic purple to white and striped types, they make a beautiful and productive crop.
Popular types:
Long Pop, Almaz, Helios (round), White Night (white), Graffiti, Matrosik (striped), long Japanese types and Black Beauty.
Spiciness
0 SHU (mild)
Maturity
100-140 days
Germination temperature
25-30C
Sensitivity
High sensitivity to cold
Step-by-step guide
1
Sowing (February - March)
Eggplants have a long season. Sow 0.5-1 cm deep. They love warmth; below 20C they may fail to germinate. Use stimulants or pre-germination on a towel.
2
Seedling care
Eggplants hate root damage. It is better to grow them without pricking out, directly in individual cups. They need 14+ hours of light and stable temperatures without drafts.
3
Hardening off (required)
This is a very tender crop. Before planting out, gradually introduce sun and fresh air. If moved straight into a greenhouse at +10C nights, plants may drop leaves.
4
Planting out (late May)
Plant in greenhouse or open ground only when night temperatures are steadily above +15C. Eggplants love rich soil with humus and plenty of sun. Spacing: 40-50 cm.
5
Watering and feeding
Use warm water only. Cold water can lead to disease. Feed with nitrogen early and potassium during flowering. Watch for Colorado potato beetles; they love eggplants.
6
Harvest (August - September)
Cut fruit with pruners when it has a glossy shine. If the skin turns matte, the eggplant is overripe and may taste bitter. Young fruit is best.
Tips for Eggplant
Pest protection: Plant near marigolds or use lightweight row cover early to deter beetles.
Cucumis Sativus (Cucumbers)
Garden sprinters! Cucumbers produce quickly but need constant care: lots of water, steady warmth and support for their tendrils. From pickling types to exotic long slicers.
Farm favorites:
Nizhynskyi (classic pickling cucumber), Tianshan (long Chinese type), Arka (reliable and productive).
Pollination
Bee-pollinated / Parthenocarpic
Maturity
38-55 days
Soil temperature
16-18C minimum
Water needs
Very high
Step-by-step guide
1
Sowing (April - May)
Cucumbers have delicate roots. Sow directly in soil or peat pots to avoid transplant shock. Depth 2 cm. Germination 3-5 days.
2
Training vines
For maximum yield, use trellises or netting. Vertical growing saves space, improves airflow and keeps fruit cleaner and healthier.
3
Watering (warm water only)
Cucumbers are about 95% water. Water regularly in the morning or evening. Cold well water can cause stress and bitterness; use settled warm water at the root.
4
Fast feeding
Cucumbers grow quickly and feed heavily. Start with nitrogen for leaves, then switch to potassium and phosphorus during flowering. They love organic matter and herbal infusions.
5
Temperature swings
If night temperature drops below +10C, cucumber growth stops. At 0C, the plant dies quickly.
6
Harvest (June - August)
The more often you pick, the more fruit grows. Do not let cucumbers overgrow and turn yellow; it drains the plant. For pickling types, 8-10 cm is ideal.
Tips for Cucumbers
Avoid bitterness: Bitterness comes from stress (drought, cold, heat). Keep soil evenly moist, not waterlogged.

CO2 boost: In a greenhouse cucumbers appreciate extra CO2; decomposing plant matter in a corner bucket can help.
Cucurbita (Pumpkins)
Heavy-hitters of the garden, from dessert-sized varieties to real giants. Pumpkins are great for cooking and autumn decor.
Farm favorites:
Uchiki Kuri (Hokkaido, sweet and edible with skin), Atlanta (potential giant), nutmeg-type pumpkins.
Storage life
4-8 months
Maturity
90-120 days
Area per plant
2-4 m2
Feeding needs
Extremely high
Step-by-step guide
1
Sowing (May)
Pumpkins dislike cold soil. Sow when soil is steadily warm (+12-15C). Depth 3-5 cm. You can transplant seedlings for earlier harvest, using individual pots.
2
A place in full sun
Choose the sunniest spot. Pumpkin is a vine and needs space. It grows beautifully on compost piles or warm beds with lots of organic matter.
3
Training and pollination
For large fruit such as Atlanta, leave 1-2 ovaries per vine. For portion-size pumpkins such as Hokkaido, you can leave more. If pollinators are scarce, hand-pollinate by moving pollen from male to female flowers.
4
Watering and support
Water deeply at the root while fruit is growing. Place small boards or straw under pumpkins so they do not rot on damp soil.
5
Harvest (before frost)
The main sign of ripeness is a dry, corky stem. Always cut with the stem attached or the pumpkin will spoil faster. Before storage, let pumpkins cure in the sun for a week.
Tips for pumpkins
Sugar concentration: Hokkaido (Uchiki Kuri) becomes sweeter a month or two after harvest. Do not rush to eat it immediately.
